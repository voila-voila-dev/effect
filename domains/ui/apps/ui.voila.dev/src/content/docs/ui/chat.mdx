The scroller keeps a reader who is at the live edge pinned to new content, holds
a turn at the reading line while a reply streams in, and preserves the position
when older history is prepended — so an arriving message never yanks the view
away from what is being read. `useChatScroller` exposes `scrollToEnd`,
`scrollToStart` and `scrollToMessage`; `useChatScrollerScrollable` reports which
edges still have content; `useChatScrollerVisibility` reports the visible rows
and the current anchored turn.

`Chat.MessageList` and its `Chat.Message*` siblings are the earlier,
self-scrolling anatomy, kept for the screens still on it. Its bubble is
`Chat.MessageBubble` (the `own`/`other` variants).

`Chat.ConversationItem` is the list-of-threads row, with `unreadCount` capped at
"99+".

## Widgets

A widget is the thing a message is about, standing under its bubble with one way
to act on it — a document to download, a record to open, a payment to confirm.
The kit knows none of them: compose the parts, and the action stretches whatever
it wraps to the widget's full width. It is sized like a bubble.

<Preview layout="stack">
	<Example.Widget />
</Preview>

```tsx
<Chat.Widget>
	<Chat.WidgetHeader>
		<Chat.WidgetIcon><FileTextIcon /></Chat.WidgetIcon>
		<Chat.WidgetHeading>
			<Chat.WidgetTitle>INV-2026-00004</Chat.WidgetTitle>
			<Chat.WidgetDetail>Issued Apr 14, 2026</Chat.WidgetDetail>
		</Chat.WidgetHeading>
	</Chat.WidgetHeader>
	<Chat.WidgetAction>
		<Button variant="outline" size="sm">Download</Button>
	</Chat.WidgetAction>
</Chat.Widget>
```

`Chat.WidgetIcon` is decorative (`aria-hidden`): the title names the thing.
